# 1. How to use this file

This is not a single website brief. It is CozyHaus's shared source of truth.

Use it when:

- building or redesigning the website;
- writing social media, ads, emails or print materials;
- briefing a photographer, designer or developer;
- training front-of-house, barista or production staff;
- describing CozyHaus to a partner, supplier or corporate customer;
- developing a new product or location;
- giving context to an AI assistant;
- deciding whether a new idea feels like CozyHaus.

This file contains three different kinds of information:

1. **Brand foundations** that should remain stable: history, purpose, values, personality and customer promise.
2. **Current strategy** that may evolve: cakes as the immediate sales priority, coffee positioning and group-order growth.
3. **Operating facts** that change more often: menu, prices, hours, ordering policies and links.

When two sections conflict, use this order:

1. The newest owner-confirmed instruction.
2. The latest operational source, such as Square or the current menu export.
3. The confirmed facts in this document.
4. Working recommendations in this document.
5. Older website or social media copy.

### Rules for AI assistants

An AI using this file must:

- never invent dates, founders, recipes, certifications, awards, customer counts or business milestones;
- distinguish confirmed fact from strategic interpretation;
- verify prices, hours, parking rules, product availability and links before publication;
- write customer-facing copy in natural Canadian English;
- use **CozyHaus** consistently, with this capitalization;
- write in a warm, direct, human voice using **we**;
- avoid em dashes, formulaic contrasts, corporate jargon and copy that sounds machine-generated;
- show confidence through specific proof, not exaggerated adjectives;
- let customer reviews support “best in Regina” claims rather than saying them in the brand's own voice;
- avoid reducing CozyHaus to an ethnic category or presenting it primarily as “an immigrant-owned café”;
- never publish internal financial, personnel or future-expansion information without explicit approval;
- use current real product and space photography wherever possible;
- treat dietary claims carefully and always disclose shared-kitchen cross-contact risk.

# 3. The core brand idea

CozyHaus brings together three things that are often separated:

- coffee treated with technical care;
- a deep, varied bakery case made largely from scratch;
- a space people genuinely want to stay in.

Many coffee shops rely on purchased pastries. Many dessert shops treat coffee as an afterthought. Many beautiful cafés are stronger in appearance than product. CozyHaus has the opportunity to own the intersection.

## 3.1 North Star

> **Make an ordinary part of someone's day feel warmer, more delicious and worth remembering.**

## 3.2 Purpose: why CozyHaus exists

CozyHaus exists to give people a comfortable place to enjoy genuinely good coffee and desserts made with care.

The purpose is not only to sell a drink or satisfy a sweet craving. The business creates a small pause in the day: a conversation, a celebration, a treat with family, an hour alone, a rainy-day refuge or a familiar place to return to.

## 3.3 Mission: what CozyHaus does every day

> Prepare balanced coffee with consistency, make distinctive desserts in-house and create an experience that leaves people happier than when they arrived.

## 3.4 Vision: where CozyHaus is going

CozyHaus aims to become one of Regina's most trusted names for coffee, desserts and celebration cakes, then continue growing without losing the product care, warm atmosphere and human service that made people return in the first place.

## 3.5 Brand promise

Customers should be able to trust that:

- the coffee has been tasted and adjusted that morning;
- the signature desserts were made by the CozyHaus team;
- the products prioritize flavour and quality ingredients over shortcuts;
- there will be enough variety for different tastes and dietary needs;
- the space will feel warm, calm and welcoming;
- if the team makes a mistake, it will try to make the moment right;
- there will be something new to discover without abandoning the favourites.

# 4. Brand history

## 4.1 Confirmed timeline

### March 2020: the home-kitchen beginning

Katie had been doing nail art from home. When COVID-19 stopped that work, she decided she needed to try something new. Macarons were highly popular in Korea at the time, but they were known for being technically difficult.

She began researching and testing macarons in March 2020.

The early process was not polished. Macaron shells repeatedly failed. The home oven was not suitable, so the family ordered another oven. It arrived with the wrong electrical connection. During the attempted return it was damaged, the refund fell through and another oven had to be ordered. The story matters because the business did not begin with a perfect plan. It began with persistence through a difficult product.

Orders came through Instagram and Facebook, with Instagram becoming the stronger channel.

The original name was **Bonjour Macaron**, chosen simply because macarons are French.

### September 2021: 82 Bakeshop opens in Saskatoon

The home business grew into a physical dessert shop in Saskatoon called **82 Bakeshop**.

The number 82 came from South Korea's international calling code, +82. It represented the founders' belief that Korea was producing some of the world's most interesting and trend-aware desserts. The name was less about serving only Korean food and more about bringing a Korean eye for new dessert trends, presentation and balanced sweetness.

### December 15, 2023: Regina opens

After the Saskatoon shop performed well, the family opened a second dessert location in Regina on December 15, 2023.

The Regina business began primarily as a takeout dessert shop. Sales were initially softer than expected. The team had not yet made full use of the location, the space or consistent local marketing.

This part of the history is useful internally: CozyHaus was not an overnight success. It grew through product improvement, a clearer concept, stronger marketing and repeat customers.

### March 2025: the café becomes CozyHaus as customers know it today

The Regina unit was roughly 1,500 square feet, but a temporary wall limited the customer-facing area to about 500 square feet. The family was already paying for the full unit.

Katie wanted to try a café. The team recognized that the unused space could become part of the product rather than remain hidden. They moved quickly, opened the full room and combined three strengths:

1. the desserts they already knew how to make;
2. coffee they genuinely wanted to drink;
3. a warm space that gave people a reason to stay.

This was the turning point. The business stopped being only a place to pick up sweets. It became a place for everyday moments.

### December 2025: the Saskatoon chapter closes

The Saskatoon location closed in December 2025 as the family simplified operations and focused its attention on its Regina businesses.

**PUBLIC NARRATIVE RULE:** This should be described as a change in focus, not as failure and not as a dramatic ending. Do not publish financial details, unit-sale information, internal workload, individual future assignments or unannounced expansion plans.

### The current chapter

CozyHaus is moving from being known mostly as a dessert shop to being understood as a complete café brand.

The next stage has three connected goals:

- become a destination for coffee in Regina;
- make the new five-cake collection a meaningful daily business;
- make group ordering easy enough that offices and teams think of CozyHaus first.

## 4.2 The story in one paragraph

> CozyHaus began at home in 2020 with a difficult batch of macarons and a decision to keep trying. What started as Instagram orders became 82 Bakeshop in Saskatoon, then a takeout dessert shop in Regina. In 2025, we opened up the rest of our space, added the coffee we had been searching for and built the warm café we had imagined. Today, CozyHaus brings together carefully prepared coffee, in-house desserts and cakes made for everyday celebrations, all in a space designed to feel like a little hideaway in the city.

# 5. Brand pillars

## Pillar 1: Coffee handled seriously, served without pretence

CozyHaus wants coffee expertise to be visible but never intimidating.

The customer does not need to understand extraction theory. They should simply notice that the latte is smooth, balanced and consistently good. For customers who want the details, CozyHaus should be proud to show the morning dial-in, tasting notes, recipe notebook, espresso machine and grinder.

**Proof:**

- espresso is tasted and dialled in before opening each day;
- dose, output and time are measured;
- grind and yield are adjusted based on taste;
- a new bag triggers an additional dial-in;
- two primary baristas lead the program, with the rest of the team being trained;
- one main espresso is used deliberately so the team can keep it consistent;
- a La Marzocco Linea Classic S 2AV and Eureka Atom W 75 support the program;
- Fratello Godfather Espresso Classic was chosen specifically for its performance in milk drinks and smooth flavour.

**Customer benefit:**

> You do not need to know why it tastes balanced. We do the work every morning so you can simply enjoy it.

## Pillar 2: An unusually deep selection of in-house desserts

The advantage is not only that desserts are made in-house. It is that CozyHaus produces an unusually wide selection in-house.

The bakery case can include macarons, cinnamon scrolls, Cookie Puffs, cookies, Basque cheesecakes, layered vegan cheesecakes, tiramisu cups, sliced cakes and rotating weekend or seasonal products.

That breadth creates discovery. A customer can return often without ordering the same thing, while reliable favourites still anchor the case.

**Approved public language:**

- In-house desserts
- Our signature desserts are made in our own kitchen
- A full pastry case made with care
- Made here, in more flavours than you can try in one visit

**Avoid as a universal literal claim:** “Everything is made from scratch” or “Every single item is made in-house.” Some minor syrups, sauces and components are purchased.

## Pillar 3: A warm hideaway in the city

Atmosphere is part of the product.

Wood, warm light, live plants, soft music and a small-scale room create the feeling of a contemporary cabin. The room should feel especially attractive on rainy, snowy, dark or cold days.

The experience should support many kinds of visits:

- a fast morning coffee;
- a lunch break;
- a quiet hour alone;
- studying or light work;
- meeting a friend;
- family dessert;
- a date;
- an evening treat when most local cafés are closed.

## Pillar 4: Care people can feel

CozyHaus wants each visit to become a happy part of the customer's day.

Care is shown through concrete actions:

- greeting customers warmly;
- explaining unfamiliar products without making people feel uninformed;
- listening carefully to dietary needs;
- remaking a drink when the team made an error or a customer accidentally ordered the wrong item, when appropriate;
- keeping the room, washroom and display areas clean;
- preparing products consistently;
- responding quickly to cake and group-order questions;
- fixing recurring feedback rather than becoming defensive.

## Pillar 5: Curious, local and always developing

CozyHaus looks outside Regina for inspiration, then does the work to create a version worth serving locally.

Examples include:

- discovering a latte at Analog Coffee in Calgary, tracing the beans to Fratello and visiting the roastery;
- travelling through Los Angeles cafés to study Einspänner, then testing a version for Regina;
- identifying Cookie Puffs and other products from Korean dessert trends;
- continuing to introduce products that are difficult to find locally.

The message is not “we copy big cities.” The message is:

> We stay curious, travel, taste and test so Regina has something new to look forward to.

# 6. Brand values

## 6.1 Craft that can be seen

Quality claims should have visible evidence. Show the notebook. Show the scale. Show the dough. Show the piping bag. Show the real ingredients. Show the imperfect testing process as well as the finished product.

## 6.2 Flavour before shortcuts

CozyHaus spends more where it improves flavour.

Examples include premium churned butter in selected recipes, real vanilla bean paste, Callebaut chocolate, Valrhona cocoa powder, premium matcha powder sourced from Korea and house-made fruit syrups.

## 6.3 Balance over excess

“Not too sweet” is intentional. In many Asian food cultures it is one of the highest compliments for dessert. CozyHaus should own this idea without implying that every product is light or low in sugar.

Approved: **Balanced sweetness. Full flavour.**

Avoid: low-sugar, healthy or guilt-free unless a specific product has verified nutritional support.

## 6.4 Consistency without rigidity

Coffee and production standards bring the product back to a trusted centre. The goal is not to make every human-made item visually identical. The goal is to keep flavour and experience within a range customers recognize.

## 6.5 Family-level care

The family regularly eats the products. That makes ingredient choices personal.

Approved expression:

> We make the kind of desserts we feel good serving our own family.

Supporting facts may include butter rather than margarine or shortening in relevant recipes, quality chocolate, real vanilla and no reliance on preservatives in the in-house dessert program.

Avoid the word **safe** as a broad nutritional or comparative claim. Avoid implying that commercial baked goods are unsafe.

## 6.6 Hospitality before ego

A technically correct drink is not enough if the customer feels unwelcome. A beautiful cake is not enough if ordering is confusing. CozyHaus succeeds when product quality and human experience work together.

## 6.7 Growth without losing warmth

Growth creates responsibility. More volume must lead to better systems, not less care. The brand can become larger while the customer experience remains personal.

# 7. Positioning

- Not only a macaron shop.

8. **Local ownership:** decisions, product development and service happen close to the customer.
9. **Trend translation:** the team brings ideas from other cities and turns them into approachable Regina products.
10. **Human service:** reviews repeatedly connect staff kindness with the desire to return.

## 7.4 The CozyHaus flywheel

1. A customer discovers a cake, product video, review or friend recommendation.
2. The website removes friction around menu, parking and hours.
3. The customer visits for coffee or a slice.
4. Product quality and atmosphere encourage a longer visit and another purchase.
5. A slice creates confidence to order a whole cake.
6. A varied order creates confidence to use CozyHaus for a team or office.
7. A good human experience creates a review, referral or repeat visit.
8. The review gives the next customer confidence.

# 8. Customer groups and jobs to be done

## 8.1 The everyday coffee customer

**Need:** a dependable latte or coffee that tastes noticeably better without requiring specialty-coffee knowledge.

**Proof to show:** daily dial-in, smooth Fratello espresso, house-made vanilla and chocolate, good milk texture and review language such as smooth and balanced.

**Primary CTA:** View the coffee menu or get directions.

## 8.2 The dessert explorer

**Need:** something new, visually interesting and difficult to find elsewhere in Regina.

**Proof to show:** a full display case, rotating flavours, Cookie Puffs, premium ingredients and behind-the-scenes production.

**Primary CTA:** See what we make.

## 8.3 The celebration buyer

**Need:** a cake that looks special, tastes reliable and is simple to order.

**Anxiety:** Will it look like the photo? Will people like the flavour? How many does it serve? When can it be picked up?

**Proof to show:** exact cake photos, slice photos, clear size and price, simple five-cake lineup, customer reviews and straightforward policies.

**Primary CTA:** Order a cake.

## 8.4 The office or group organizer

**Need:** one easy order that works for different tastes and dietary needs.

**Anxiety:** minimums, notice, allergies, pickup logistics, invoice and response time.

**Proof to show:** sample assortments, dietary mix, $150 threshold, 15% discount, response expectations and clear inquiry fields.

**Primary CTA:** Request a group order.

## 8.5 The dietary-needs customer

**Need:** a dessert option that does not feel like a lesser substitute.

**Proof to show:** vegan layered cheesecakes and cookies that non-vegan customers also praise, plus honest shared-kitchen disclosure.

**Primary CTA:** View vegan and gluten-free options.

## 8.6 Friends, couples and families looking for a place

**Need:** somewhere warm to talk, rest or spend time, especially later in the day.

**Proof to show:** full-room images, warm lighting, real plants, quiet music, seating moments and late hours.

**Primary CTA:** Plan your visit.

# 10. Coffee program

## 10.1 Coffee positioning

CozyHaus is not trying to become a technical coffee laboratory for a small group of experts. It is building an approachable specialty-coffee reputation for the broad middle of Regina: customers who may not use coffee vocabulary but can immediately tell when a latte is smooth, balanced and satisfying.

The desired reaction is:

> “I do not know exactly what they do differently, but the latte here is really good.”

The website and social content can explain the difference without making the customer work to understand it.

## 10.2 Espresso

**CONFIRMED / PUBLIC**

- Coffee: Fratello Coffee Roasters, Godfather Espresso Classic
- Roast: medium
- Origins: Guatemala Montecristo, Colombian Bolivar, Nicaraguan Los Placeres and Peru Organic COEX
- Tasting notes: milk chocolate and caramel

- Best fit: straight espresso and milk-based drinks, especially lattes

## 10.3 Why this coffee

The origin story is more important than the supplier logo alone.

The family was preparing to build a café and tried a latte at Analog Coffee on Calgary's 17th Avenue. The drink was exceptionally smooth, with no harsh or distracting note. It felt like the answer to the kind of latte CozyHaus wanted to serve.

They researched the coffee, learned it came from Fratello, contacted the roaster and visited the facility to taste and understand it in person. The decision was not based on convenience. It began with a cup they genuinely loved.

### Approved public copy

> **We found our coffee in Calgary.**
>
> A latte at Analog Coffee was so smooth and balanced that we needed to know what was behind it. That search led us to Fratello Coffee Roasters. We visited the roastery, tasted the coffee and chose Godfather Espresso Classic for the same reason we noticed it in the first place: milk chocolate and caramel notes, a rich body and a flavour that works beautifully in a latte.

## 10.4 The daily dial-in

**CONFIRMED / PUBLIC**

Before opening, the barista:

1. pulls and tastes the existing espresso recipe;
2. measures the coffee dose;
3. measures the espresso yield;
4. checks extraction time;
5. evaluates the taste;
6. adjusts grind size or yield when needed;
7. confirms that the coffee has returned to the desired flavour range.

The normal routine happens once before opening. A new bag of beans triggers another dial-in.

Weather, humidity and the changing condition of coffee can affect extraction. The setting that worked yesterday may not produce the same cup today.

### Approved explanation

> **Yesterday's grinder setting is not automatically today's.**
>
> We taste our espresso before opening, check the dose, yield and time, then adjust it when needed. It is a small routine, but it is how we keep the coffee centred on the smooth, balanced flavour we want you to taste.

## 10.5 Why one main espresso is a strength

Customers sometimes ask for more bean choices. CozyHaus currently chooses depth over variety.

One espresso still changes with the weather, humidity, age of the beans and each new bag. Keeping one main espresso allows the team to know it deeply and protect consistency.

### Approved public copy

> **One espresso. Dialled every morning.**
>
> We keep one main espresso because consistency takes attention. Coffee changes with time and weather, so we taste it each morning and adjust the recipe when needed. More choices may come later. Right now, we would rather know one coffee well and serve it properly.

### Welcome

- Treat atmosphere as part of service.

- Follow the morning coffee calibration.
- Record material recipe or process changes.
- Protect portion and presentation standards.
- Treat feedback patterns as information for improvement.

## 19.3 Internal training principle

The customer should never need to see the effort behind a smooth visit, but the team should understand that the effort is the brand.

# 20. Brand voice

## 20.1 Personality

CozyHaus sounds:

- warm;
- friendly;
- observant;
- quietly confident;
- straightforward;
- curious;
- human;
- grateful without becoming repetitive or sentimental.

## 20.2 Writing rules

- Write in first-person plural: **we**.
- Use short, natural sentences.
- Prefer one specific detail over three adjectives.
- Use Canadian English spelling.
- Sound like an owner speaking honestly, not a brand committee.
- Confidence is welcome when a real process or ingredient supports it.
- Use humour lightly and naturally.
- Use emotional language when the story has a real moment behind it.
- Use calls to action that are easy and direct.
- Avoid excessive emojis on the website.
- Avoid em dashes in social copy and customer-facing prose.

## 20.3 Voice examples

### Good

> We taste our espresso every morning before we serve it.

> The classic Cinnamon Scroll still sells the most. We understand why.

> It started with macarons and an oven that did not cooperate.

> Try a slice today. Bring home the whole cake for your next celebration.

> Parking is easier than it looks.

### Avoid

> Embark on an unparalleled artisanal journey.

> Where passion meets perfection.

> More than coffee. A lifestyle.

> Elevate your celebration with our exquisite creations.

> We are redefining Regina's culinary landscape.

The avoided examples are generic, inflated and easily mistaken for AI-generated marketing.

## 20.4 Owner-story voice

The strongest social writing often begins with a real, ordinary moment:

- the milk ran out during a rush;
- a staff member called in sick;
- an early macaron batch failed;
- a customer said the atmosphere felt good at the end of a difficult day;
- the team travelled to taste a new drink;
- a child insisted on photo credit;
- growth felt exciting and heavy at the same time.

The owner voice can admit effort, uncertainty and responsibility. It should end with what the moment revealed, not a forced motivational lesson.

## 20.5 Message order

For most promotional copy:

1. Human hook or direct benefit
2. Specific product or process detail
3. Why it matters to the customer
4. One clear action
5. Hours and location when relevant

Total sales cannot be credited only to the website. Social media, reviews, word of mouth, repeat customers and physical visibility all contribute.

Use direction, order and inquiry events as leading indicators. Use sales and review-language shifts as broader outcome indicators.

# 33. Visual identity direction

## 33.1 Creative direction

> **Modern cabin, not themed cabin.**

The design should feel warm, natural and contemporary. It should not use obvious German motifs, chalet clichés or rustic stock imagery.

## 33.2 Visual principles

- Use the current warm green as an anchor.
- Pair green with warm cream, wood brown, muted berry and restrained charcoal.
- Let food and interior photography carry colour.
- Use generous space, but do not make the site feel cold or luxury-exclusive.
- Mix clean typography with one subtle human or handwritten accent only if highly legible.
- Use texture sparingly: paper, wood grain or hand-drawn marks can reference the cake menu illustration.
- Keep buttons and product information crisp and modern.
- Use rounded or organic image framing only when it supports the logo and natural atmosphere.
- Prefer editorial composition over a grid of identical cards.
- Avoid excessive animation.

## 33.3 Recommended colour roles

Exact colour values must be sampled from the logo and approved during design.

- Warm green: brand anchor, buttons, links and key headings
- Warm cream: main page background
- Dark espresso brown or charcoal: text
- Natural wood: photography and subtle dividers
- Berry or muted red: limited cake and seasonal accent
- Soft sage: secondary backgrounds

## 33.4 Typography direction

- Main typeface: highly readable contemporary sans serif
- Optional accent: restrained handwritten or soft serif style
- Product names and prices must remain highly readable
- Do not use a handwritten font for body copy, menus, policies or accessibility-critical information

## 33.5 Image direction

Photography should make three things visible:

1. the care in the process;
2. the abundance and texture of the products;
3. the feeling of being in the room.

### Avoid

- generic stock café photography;
- fake steam or over-edited food;
- inconsistent old and new cake designs on the same sales page;
- isolated product cutouts as the main atmosphere image;
- wide empty-room images with no emotional focal point;
- dark images that hide product colour;
- close-ups so tight that customers cannot judge size.

## 33.6 Website motion

Use motion only to support discovery:

- subtle reveal as cake cards enter;
- gentle horizontal product gallery on mobile;
- small hover movement on desktop;
- no scroll hijacking;
- no autoplay audio;
- respect reduced-motion preferences;
- no motion that delays reading or ordering.

# 36. Product-development filter

Before a new product becomes part of CozyHaus, ask:

1. Does it taste good enough to recommend personally?
2. Is it different enough to give customers a reason to notice?
3. Can the team make it consistently?
4. Does the ingredient cost improve the customer's experience?
5. Can it be explained simply?
6. Does it photograph well without relying on appearance alone?
7. Does it fit the existing coffee, dessert, cake or lunch system?
8. Can it survive real production volume?
9. Does it create a reason to return?
10. Is it permanent, seasonal, weekend-only or a test?

The answer does not need to be yes to every question, but the role of the product must be clear.

# 37. Website quality requirements

## 37.1 Mobile first

Most discovery from social media and local search will occur on a phone.

- Hero text and Cake Order button must fit without visual clutter.
- Menus must not require pinch zoom.
- Phone, directions and cake actions must be easy to tap.
- Group-order forms should use appropriate mobile input types.
- Cake photography must crop safely on narrow screens.

## 37.2 Performance

- Serve appropriately sized WebP or AVIF images with fallbacks where needed.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold images.
- Do not lazy-load the hero image.
- Reserve image dimensions to avoid page shift.
- Use limited font weights.
- Avoid heavy animation libraries for decorative effects.
- Test Core Web Vitals on real mobile connections.

## 37.3 Accessibility

- Meet WCAG 2.2 AA colour contrast and interaction expectations.
- Provide keyboard access.
- Use visible focus states.
- Use semantic headings.
- Label forms and errors clearly.
- Provide alt text that describes the useful image content.
- Do not put menu information only in images.
- Respect reduced motion.
- Make tap targets large enough.
- Do not use colour alone for dietary labels or parking zones.

## 37.4 Trust and legal basics

- Show current privacy information for forms and analytics.
- Collect only information needed for the inquiry.
- Protect form submissions from spam.
- Do not expose customer data in analytics.
- Present cake cancellation terms before outbound checkout where possible.
- Keep allergen and cross-contact language visible.
- Add copyright and business contact information.
